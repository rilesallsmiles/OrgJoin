Our approach

Fix the interfaces.
Keep the momentum.

Growing companies rarely fail for lack of talented people. Execution breaks where teams, decisions, systems, and accountability meet. We find those seams, design the right connections, and help make them work in practice.

The operating premise

The goal is not more structure. It is less friction between the parts that already exist.

As organizations grow, informal coordination stops scaling. Decisions travel upward, ownership becomes ambiguous, and leaders become the connection between functions that should work together directly.

Adding meetings, policies, or another tool can make the problem worse. The useful question is: which connection is failing, and what is the smallest durable mechanism that will repair it?

That is where Organizational Joinery begins.

The method

Diagnose. Design. Install.

Three connected stages, with the work grounded in how the organization actually operates—not how its org chart says it should.

01 / DIAGNOSE

See the seams.

Trace real work and decisions across functional boundaries. Find where people are compensating for unclear interfaces.

  • Interview the people doing and routing the work
  • Follow decisions, handoffs, and escalations
  • Separate symptoms from structural causes
  • Identify the few constraints that matter most
02 / DESIGN

Build the joint.

Translate what we learn into practical operating mechanisms, proportionate to the company's stage and complexity.

  • Clarify ownership and decision authority
  • Define cross-functional interfaces and escalation rules
  • Align workflows, information, and operating cadence
  • Fit the mechanism to tools already in use
03 / INSTALL

Make it hold.

Work alongside the team to test the mechanism in real conditions, adjust what fails, and transfer ownership.

  • Test with real scenarios and edge cases
  • Support implementation and adoption
  • Document the decisions and operating rules
  • Hand off a system the team can run independently

Design principles

Enough structure to scale.
Not enough to slow you down.

01

Decisions before boxes.

Start with what must be decided, by whom, and under what conditions. Reporting lines alone do not define effective authority.

02

Make interfaces explicit.

Accountability, consultation, handoffs, and escalation should be visible where functions meet—not left to personal relationships.

03

Use the systems you have.

Prefer improvements that live in existing workflows and platforms. A new tool is not the default answer.

04

Design for independence.

A good operating mechanism survives changes in people, reporting lines, and growth. It should not require its designer to remain in the room.
